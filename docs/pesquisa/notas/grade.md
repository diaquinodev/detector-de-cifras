# Bar grid and chord hold

Scope: how a chord chart stays on one symbol while the spectrum flickers, and how a change is forced onto a downbeat. Chordino the Vamp plugin, madmom’s beat trackers, and essentia’s chord and rhythm algorithms are separate stages. The bar lock lives in a second model (or in a hand-written prior), not inside the frame-wise chord classifier.

Paper claims are marked as such. GitHub comments, the 2017 MIR blog, and Music Stack Exchange answers are anecdotes.

## How do Chordino, madmom, and essentia estimate beat, downbeat, and meter separately from the chord label?

### Takeaway
None of the three chord labelers builds the bar grid from the chord label. Chordino does not estimate beat, downbeat, or meter at all: it decodes a frame-wise chord HMM from bass and treble chroma. madmom’s beat and downbeat trackers read a trained activation (an RNN on the magnitude spectrogram, or, in the older pattern tracker, spectral flux) and decode tempo and bar position with their own HMM. essentia’s `RhythmExtractor2013` returns beat times and BPM only; downbeat and meter are absent, and `ChordsDetectionBeats` consumes beat times as an external input.

### Cited Findings
- Chordino’s own description says chord profiles in `chord.dict` are matched to frame-wise NNLS chroma and then smoothed by HMM/Viterbi. It does not mention beats, downbeats, or meter. — [c4dm/nnls-chroma README](https://github.com/c4dm/nnls-chroma); [Chordino.cpp](https://github.com/c4dm/nnls-chroma/blob/master/Chordino.cpp)
- In `Chordino.cpp` the chord score is a dot product of a 24-bin vector (12 bass chroma stacked with 12 treble chroma) against each dictionary row. The last dictionary state is scaled by 0.7 before a `pow(1.3, ·)` and a sum-to-one normalisation. That is a harmonic match, not an onset, bass-onset, or harmonic-change detector. — [Chordino.cpp](https://github.com/c4dm/nnls-chroma/blob/master/Chordino.cpp)
- The plugin’s advertised frame rate is `sampleRate/2048` when `stepSize` is 0, else `sampleRate/stepSize`. At 44.1 kHz and the 2048-sample fallback that is about 21.5 frames per second. — [Chordino.cpp](https://github.com/c4dm/nnls-chroma/blob/master/Chordino.cpp)
- A fork README lists the user knobs that do exist: HMM on by default, spectral whitening default 1.0, spectral shape default 0.7, chroma normalisation default none, “boost likelihood of the N label” default 0.1. No beat or meter knob. — [shidephen/chordino README](https://github.com/shidephen/chordino)
- The research system that does lock chords to a bar is not the Chordino plugin. Mauch’s thesis uses a six-layer dynamic Bayesian network whose hidden nodes are metric position, key, chord, and bass pitch class, with observed bass chroma and treble chroma. Beats are the time base; metric position is a 4-state chain, not something read off the chord label. — [Mauch PhD thesis, Ch. 4](https://webspace.eecs.qmul.ac.uk/s.e.dixon/phd/MatthiasMauch-PhD-Thesis.pdf); summary in [Dixon, Mauch, and Anglade, LNCS 6684](https://webspace.eecs.qmul.ac.uk/s.e.dixon/pub/2011/Dixon-Mauch-Anglade-LNCS6684.pdf)
- That meter chain is: with ε = 0.05, `P(m_i | m_{i-1}) = 1 − ε` if the position advances by one beat modulo 4, `ε/2` if it stays or skips one beat, and 0 if it jumps by three. Mauch says the ideal (ε = 0) version is the model of Papadopoulos and Peeters (2008). — [Mauch thesis, eq. 4.1](https://webspace.eecs.qmul.ac.uk/s.e.dixon/phd/MatthiasMauch-PhD-Thesis.pdf)
- Key is a separate 24-state chain with self-transition 0.98 and `(1 − 0.98)/23` to every other key. — [Mauch thesis, eq. 4.2](https://webspace.eecs.qmul.ac.uk/s.e.dixon/phd/MatthiasMauch-PhD-Thesis.pdf)
- Bass is also separate. On the first beat of a new chord the nominal chord bass has probability 0.8 and each of the other 12 pitch classes shares 0.2. Mauch’s count for that 80% figure: 20 of 207 tutorial bass patterns in Westwood (1997) do not start on the chord bass. While the chord is held, the 0.8 mass is shared by the chord tones, with the nominal bass keeping double weight. — [Mauch thesis, eq. 4.8](https://webspace.eecs.qmul.ac.uk/s.e.dixon/phd/MatthiasMauch-PhD-Thesis.pdf)
- de Haas, Magalhães, and Wiering (2012) likewise keep the grid outside the chord model: NNLS chroma from Mauch’s Vamp plugin, beats and “position of the beat inside the bar” from the Queen Mary Bar and Beat Tracker (Davies and Plumbley, 2007). Chroma is then averaged between beats. This paper does not re-specify whether that beat tracker uses spectral flux, harmonic change, or bass; it treats the plugin as an external clock. — [ISMIR 2012, §3.1–3.2](https://ismir2012.ismir.net/event/papers/295_ISMIR_2012.pdf)
- madmom `DBNBeatTrackingProcessor` tracks beats only. Defaults in `beats.py`: `min_bpm=55`, `max_bpm=215`, `num_tempi=None`, `transition_lambda=100`, `observation_lambda=16`, `threshold=0`, `correct=True`. Observations are an RNN beat activation, not chroma and not a chord label. The decoder is an HMM: one beat period is split into `observation_lambda` phase bins; the first bin is the beat state. `transition_lambda` is the rate of an exponential tempo-change distribution; larger values prefer a constant tempo. — [madmom/features/beats.py](https://github.com/CPJKU/madmom/blob/main/madmom/features/beats.py); [beats_hmm docs](https://madmom.readthedocs.io/en/v0.16.1/modules/features/beats_hmm.html)
- A NumPy reimplementation of that decoder (given activations, not instead of them) is `log((1 − a) / (observation_lambda − 1))` for non-beat states and `log(a)` for the beat state, with beat states those whose phase is `< 1/observation_lambda`. — [compiam dbn_beat_tracking](https://mtg.github.io/compIAM/_modules/compiam/utils/dbn.html)
- madmom `DBNDownBeatTrackingProcessor` jointly tracks beats and downbeats. Documented defaults: `min_bpm=55`, `max_bpm=215`, `num_tempi=60`, `transition_lambda=100`, `observation_lambda=16`, `threshold=0.05`, `correct=True`, and a required `beats_per_bar` (an int or a list of bar lengths). Output is time in seconds plus position inside the bar, counting from 1. Same `transition_lambda` meaning as the beat tracker. — [madmom downbeats docs](https://madmom.readthedocs.io/en/v0.16.1/modules/features/downbeats.html)
- Böck, Krebs, and Widmer (ISMIR 2016), the paper those two processors implement, state that the RNN reads magnitude spectrograms and classifies each frame as beat, downbeat, or neither, and that the DBN then infers meter, tempo, and phase. They explicitly say the system avoids hand-crafted harmonic-change detectors and rhythmic patterns. The published limitation is that the meter cannot change inside a piece, because the state space would be too large. — [Böck, Krebs, and Widmer, ISMIR 2016](https://www.cp.jku.at/research/papers/Boeck_etal_ISMIR_2016.pdf)
- An older madmom downbeat path, described on an essentia issue, is different: `PatternTrackingProcessor` / `GMMPatternTracker` uses a spectral-flux onset feature and pre-trained per-meter GMM rhythmic patterns, then Viterbi over a joint bar–tempo state space. That is onset energy, still not a chord label, and it still needs the shipped pattern models. — [MTG/essentia issue 253](https://github.com/MTG/essentia/issues/253)
- essentia `RhythmExtractor2013` inputs the waveform and outputs `bpm`, beat `ticks` in seconds, `confidence`, a list of BPM `estimates`, and `bpmIntervals`. Parameters: `method` in `{multifeature, degara}` default `multifeature`, `minTempo` default 40 (range 40–180), `maxTempo` default 208 (range 60–250). It requires 44100 Hz. The docs do not list a downbeat or a time-signature output. `degara` always returns confidence 0. The tutorial says the algorithm uses statistics over the whole track and is not for real time, and that the reported BPM is the average of the per-interval estimates. — [RhythmExtractor2013](https://essentia.upf.edu/reference/std_RhythmExtractor2013.html); [beat-detection tutorial](https://essentia.upf.edu/tutorial_rhythm_beatdetection.html)
- Onset detection is a separate essentia stage. `OnsetDetectionGlobal` offers `infogain` (default; frame 2048, hop 512, 44100 Hz) and `beat_emphasis` (complex spectral difference in sub-bands, weighted by a whole-signal beat strength; optimised for frame 1024, hop 512). Peaks of that function are note or percussive onsets, not chord changes. — [OnsetDetectionGlobal](https://essentia.upf.edu/reference/std_OnsetDetectionGlobal.html)
- Maintainer statement, not a paper: Dmitry Bogdanov, 11 May 2015, on essentia issue 253: downbeat detection “is not possible with the algorithms we currently have”; for 4/4, the first tick of `RhythmExtractor2013` “will often correspond to the first downbeat, but we did not evaluate that.” — [MTG/essentia issue 253](https://github.com/MTG/essentia/issues/253)
- `ChordsDetection` never sees beats. It averages HPCP frames in a centered window (`windowSize` default 2 seconds, `hopSize` default 2048, `sampleRate` default 44100) and picks the best major or minor triad. One label per HPCP frame. Docs: “experimental (prone to errors)”. — [ChordsDetection](https://essentia.upf.edu/reference/std_ChordsDetection.html); [chordsdetection.cpp](https://github.com/MTG/essentia/blob/master/src/algorithms/tonal/chordsdetection.cpp)
- `ChordsDetectionBeats` takes HPCP plus `ticks`. It emits exactly one chord per interval between adjacent ticks. Default `chromaPick=interbeat_median` (per-bin median of HPCP frames in the interval, then L1-style normalisation in the source); the alternative `starting_beat` copies the HPCP frame at the left tick. Fewer than 2 ticks throws. Ticks past the end of the HPCP are dropped. Major becomes the note name, minor becomes the name plus `m`. There is no self-transition and no downbeat test, so nothing in this algorithm holds a label across a tick if the next median matches a different triad. — [ChordsDetectionBeats](https://essentia.upf.edu/reference/std_ChordsDetectionBeats.html); [chordsdetectionbeats.cpp](https://github.com/MTG/essentia/blob/v2.1_beta5-1445-gb9fa6cb6/src/algorithms/tonal/chordsdetectionbeats.cpp)

### Inferences
- The behaviour “change on the downbeat, hold while the spectrum flickers” is two mechanisms stacked. The hold is a duration prior (Chordino’s 0.99 self-loop, or a beat-wise stay probability). The downbeat alignment is a separate clock (madmom’s bar-position HMM, the QM bar tracker, or Mauch’s 4-state meter) plus a prior that puts almost all of the change mass on positions 1 and 3. Feeding chord labels back in as the onset function is not what these systems do.
- NumPy and SciPy are enough for: a spectral-flux or complex-difference onset curve; a uniform-transition chord HMM and Viterbi; a Hanning or median smooth of chroma; and, once beat times and a 4/4 phase are already known, Mauch’s metric-position weights. They are not enough for madmom’s RNN activations or for the shipped GMM bar patterns. essentia’s `multifeature` / `degara` trackers are library algorithms with their own internal models, not a few array operations. A NumPy port can decode a DBN only after something else has produced the activation.

### Gaps
- This pass did not re-read Davies and Plumbley (2007), so the exact onset feature inside the Queen Mary Bar and Beat Tracker is not restated here. The 2012 chord paper only says it supplies beat times and beat-in-bar position.
- essentia’s `BeatTrackerMultiFeature` feature list was not opened beyond the `RhythmExtractor2013` wrapper. The wrapper docs name the method, not the onset channels.
- No current Chordino source path estimates meter. Anyone who wants a Chordino chart on barlines has to run a beat/downbeat tracker beside it.

## How do HMM, Viterbi, and HarmTrace-style grammars decide when a chord may change?

### Takeaway
Three published mechanisms, with different numbers. Chordino’s Viterbi uses one hardcoded self-transition, 0.99, and a uniform probability on every other chord; it has no beat strength. Mauch’s beat-synchronous DBN uses change weights 0.5, 0.1, 0.4, 0.1 on beats 1–4 of a 4/4 bar, over a 121-chord vocabulary in the thesis experiments (25 and 49 in the smaller ones). HarmTrace, in the 2012 audio system, does not use a self-loop probability: it merges neighbouring beat-level candidate lists when they intersect, and it only starts a merge on beat 1 or beat 3.

### Cited Findings
- Chordino transition, copied from the plugin (this is source, not a tuned-on-data claim):

```text
selftransprob = 0.99
init = 0 everywhere, 1 on the last state   # the N slot
trans[i, j] = selftransprob                    if i == j
            = (1 - selftransprob) / (nChord - 1)  otherwise
path = Viterbi(init, trans, chordogram)
```

  A new label is emitted only when `path[t] != path[t-1]`. A side output sums `num * log(num/denom)` over chords from the Viterbi deltas; that is a change score, not a second decoder. — [Chordino.cpp](https://github.com/c4dm/nnls-chroma/blob/master/Chordino.cpp)
- The fallback template list in `chromamethods.cpp` (revision `9d81703dcf6e`) contains 18 bass/treble profiles (major, minor, dim7, 6, 7, maj7, m7, m6, dim, aug, 7sus4, and several slash/inversion rows), each expanded over 12 roots by `chordDictionary`. An external `chord.dict` replaces that list. The 2010 dictionary diff shows the same families, including a `7sus4` line. This pass did not re-count the expanded state total on current master, so “18 types × 12, plus whatever the loader appends for N” is the safe statement. The Viterbi code treats the last state as the special one (×0.7 salience, initial probability 1). — [chromamethods.cpp](https://code.soundsoftware.ac.uk/hg/nnls-chroma/file/9d81703dcf6e/chromamethods.cpp); [chord.dict revision note](https://code.soundsoftware.ac.uk/hg/nnls-chroma/rev/e72c4ad432d8?revcount=30&style=paper)
- Mauch and Dixon’s plain (no meter) duration model is a negative binomial with shape 2 and scale 1/3, “corresponding to an expected chord duration of 4 beats.” That is the hold-one-bar prior before metric position is switched on. — [Mauch thesis, §4.3.1](https://webspace.eecs.qmul.ac.uk/s.e.dixon/phd/MatthiasMauch-PhD-Thesis.pdf)
- With meter on, the chord change weights are the vector `a = (0.5, 0.1, 0.4, 0.1)` for metric positions 1, 2, 3, 4. For `N_C` chords:

```text
P(c_i | c_{i-1}, m_i) = 1 - a[m_i]                 if c_i == c_{i-1}
                      = a[m_i] / (N_C - 1)         otherwise
```

  Stay probabilities are therefore 0.5, 0.9, 0.6, 0.9. The thesis states the musical claim in words: a change is likely at the start of the bar, less likely on beat 3, and less likely again on beats 2 and 4. The same `a` is used for every chord type; chord identity is handled by a separate key factor. — [Mauch thesis, eqs. 4.3–4.5](https://webspace.eecs.qmul.ac.uk/s.e.dixon/phd/MatthiasMauch-PhD-Thesis.pdf)
- Thesis vocabularies actually decoded: `majmin` = 25 (12 major + 12 minor + N), `inv` = 49 (those plus `maj/3` and `maj/5`), `full` = 121 (`maj`, `min`, `maj/3`, `maj/5`, `maj6`, `7`, `maj7`, `min7`, `dim`, `aug`, and N). Viterbi via Bayes Net Toolbox `find_mpe`. On 210 songs (MIREX 2009 chord set: 174 Beatles, 18 Queen, 18 Zweieck), adding metric position, bass, and key each raised overlap; the step from plain to metric-only was the one addition that was not significant under Tukey–Kramer at 95% after a Friedman test. — [Mauch thesis, §4.3](https://webspace.eecs.qmul.ac.uk/s.e.dixon/phd/MatthiasMauch-PhD-Thesis.pdf)
- The IEEE TASLP abstract of the same project says “109 different chords” and 71% on the 176-track MIREX 2008 set, and says bass and meter modelling help segmentation granularity. The thesis experiment writes the inventory as 121. Both figures are in print; they are not the same count. — [IEEE TASLP 2010 abstract](https://doi.org/10.1109/tasl.2009.2032947); [Mauch thesis, §4.3.1](https://webspace.eecs.qmul.ac.uk/s.e.dixon/phd/MatthiasMauch-PhD-Thesis.pdf)
- Earlier Mauch and Dixon (ISMIR 2008) already fought one-frame chords. A single HMM state has an exponential duration, which piles probability on very short chords; their Beatles histogram has the mass between about 1 and 3 seconds and none on the shortest bins. They replace one state by three identical-emission left-to-right states so duration is gamma with shape k = 3. Scale `λ̂ = d̄ / k` from the mean chord duration, and each intra-chord self-transition is `1 − 1/λ̂`. Between-chord transitions are bigram counts from symbolic data, smoothed by adding the maximum count. Vocabulary: 6 qualities × 12 = 72 labels. The gamma model kept frame accuracy and cut fragmentation. — [ISMIR 2008](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2008/paper/000214.pdf)
- Cho, Weiss, and Bello (SMC 2010) compared transition matrices at a fast frame rate (they illustrate chroma hops of 1 s vs 96 ms). Their conclusion, in the paper’s own wording: optimal post-filtering raised accuracy by a little under 30 points “with high self-transition probabilities (determined by the parameter P) being entirely responsible,” and the choice among a uniform matrix, a Beatles bigram matrix, and a circle-of-fifths matrix was negligible once that penalty was set. Pre-filtering (moving-average length N) helped by as much as about 20 points; the best combined setting they describe uses little pre-filtering (`N = 3`) plus a model-specific P. A figure shows the matrices after `P = 2.0`. They also warn that these rankings are for fast fixed-rate frames on a small homogeneous set, and that beat segmentation would change the picture but can propagate beat errors. The OCR of their parameter table is not clean enough to quote per-model P values from this pass. — [Cho, Weiss, and Bello, SMC 2010](https://ronw.net/pubs/smc2010-chordreco.pdf)
- Müller and Zalkow’s FMP notebook states the same mechanism without a production constant: a uniform matrix with a large diagonal changes chord only when the emission gain pays for the small off-diagonal. Broken-chord flicker dies; the large harmonic changes remain. The notebook is a NumPy/SciPy implementation of that idea, not a fitted self-loop for pop audio. — [FMP C5S3](https://www.audiolabs-erlangen.de/resources/MIR/FMP/C5/C5S3_ChordRec_HMM.html)
- de Haas et al. (ISMIR 2012) dictionary: major, minor, and dominant seventh, rotated to 12 roots = 48 chords, plus an all-zero “no chord.” Bass is a separate one-hot 12-vector. Candidates at a beat are ranked by Euclidean distance of the binary template to beat-averaged chroma; distances are divided by the best distance so the best candidate scores 1. A candidate list is cut at 0.9 (chosen “after some experimentation”). — [ISMIR 2012, §3.2](https://ismir2012.ismir.net/event/papers/295_ISMIR_2012.pdf)
- Their grammar does not score a self-loop. It merges two adjacent candidate lists by intersection when the intersection is non-empty, and the left list must sit on beat 1 or beat 3. Merged lists may merge onward, so a bar can collapse to one chord. Lists are then split at key changes and at beats where a I or V degree is present, and a leftover sequence is capped at 12 chords and at 30 expanded sequences. For each segment the error-correcting HarmTrace parser keeps the sequence with the lowest insertion/deletion ratio, then the smallest tree, then the shallowest tree. Key smoothing uses modulation penalty `p = 1` and a minimum key span of 16 beats. — [ISMIR 2012, §3.4–3.6](https://ismir2012.ismir.net/event/papers/295_ISMIR_2012.pdf)
- On 217 songs (179 Beatles, 20 Queen, 18 Zweieck), relative correct overlap was SIMPLE 0.688, GROUP (merge only) 0.736, MPTREE 0.739, MPTREEkey (ground-truth keys) 0.741. Friedman `χ²(3, N=217) = 339`, `p < 0.0001`. GROUP vs MPTREE was not significant. The paper’s own conclusion: most of the gain is “forcing chords not to change often and mainly at strong metrical positions,” not the jazz grammar. For the MIREX 25-class score they map both major and dominant-seventh labels onto major, and minor onto minor. — [ISMIR 2012, §4–5](https://ismir2012.ismir.net/event/papers/295_ISMIR_2012.pdf)
- HarmTrace itself is a Haskell grammar over symbolic labels (Harte syntax plus a duration). The parser stores a default duration of 1, and a beat marker of `1`, `2`, `3`, `4`, or `x`. It explains a sequence; it does not hear audio. — [HarmTrace-Base ChordParser](https://hackage.haskell.org/package/HarmTrace-Base-1.6.0.0/docs/src/HarmTrace-Base-Parse-ChordParser.html); [haas/harmtrace](https://github.com/haas/harmtrace)

### Inferences
- At Chordino’s ~21.5 Hz, a flat emission and a 0.99 self-loop imply a geometric run of mean 100 frames, about 4.6 s, before a change. Real emissions are not flat, so chords change sooner; the 0.99 is a reluctance, not a bar lock. Mauch’s beat-wise stays (0.5 / 0.9 / 0.6 / 0.9) are the version that knows which beat it is on. Chordino can hold through spectral flicker and still change in the middle of a bar, because every frame is exchangeable.
- For `N_C = 121`, Mauch’s probability of one particular other chord on the downbeat is `0.5/120 ≈ 0.0042`, against a stay of 0.5. On beat 2 or 4 the stay is 0.9 and one rival gets `0.1/120 ≈ 0.00083`. A flicker has to be a much better chroma match on a weak beat than on beat 1 or beat 3.
- Cho, Weiss, and Bello’s result is why copying a “musical” bigram matrix rarely fixes a chart that changes every frame: at a hop much shorter than a chord, the diagonal swamps the musical off-diagonal. The musical matrix starts to matter after the time base is a beat, which is the regime of Mauch and of de Haas.
- NumPy/SciPy can implement Chordino’s matrix, Mauch’s `a` vector (beats supplied), the 2008 three-state duration trick, and the FMP uniform HMM. HarmTrace’s error-correcting grammar is a Haskell parser, not an array program. The 121-state DBN in the thesis was MATLAB BNT and peaked at 6–15 GB; that is a faithful implementation note, not a requirement to copy it.

### Gaps
- Chordino’s 0.99 is a literal in `Chordino.cpp`. This pass found no paper in which Mauch reports that constant as the result of a grid search.
- Per-model optimal `P` numbers in Cho, Weiss, and Bello’s Table 3 were not reliably readable from the text extraction (one line shows a scatter of integers and a 1.5). They are omitted rather than guessed.
- The IEEE abstract’s 109 and the thesis’s 121 were not reconciled against a third table. Use 121 when implementing the thesis experiment; cite 109 only as the journal abstract’s count.

## What do practitioners report when every beat gets a new chord?

### Takeaway
The reports that name a number do not say “raise the neural-net threshold.” They say: stop classifying raw inter-beat medians; smooth chroma for about 1–2 seconds; put a stay probability in a Viterbi pass; and, if the change is merely late, snap the boundary to the next beat or to the downbeat. A peaked root-transition matrix without a strong stay made one Beatles experiment worse (80% region down to 48%).

### Cited Findings
- Paper, not a forum. Zenz and Rauber (described on the IFS page and in the ICSPC 2007 extract) split audio at beats from BeatRoot, whose onset stage is spectral flux, then tempo induction from inter-onset intervals, then competing agents. PCP used a 1024-sample Hamming window at 22050 Hz (46.4 ms) and a 512-sample hop (23.2 ms). The chord smoother “rates each chord according to the number of chord changes around it” and keeps the highest score. Their stated reason for beats: chord changes “usually occur at beat time,” so the analysis block can be longer without straddling two chords. — [IFS chord-detection page](https://www.ifs.tuwien.ac.at/mir/chorddetection.html); [ICSPC 2007 extract](http://www.ifs.tuwien.ac.at/mir/pub/zenz_icspc2007)
- Blog write-up of a 56-song Beatles subset, with a public script repo. This is an experiment log, not a peer-reviewed result. Essentia `ChordsDetectionBeats` on 4-beat segments, or `ChordsDetection` with 2-second windows, scored 61%. NNLS chroma in place of HPCP: 69.6%. Hanning smooth of length 2 seconds, then one sample per beat (no inter-beat median): 76.2%. Swapping `BeatTrackerMultiFeature` for madmom DBN beats: 76.7% (almost no gain on Beatles; the author says madmom helped more on jazz). Softmax of template correlations into an HMM: 80.5%. — [Eremenko, 21 June 2017](https://musicinformationretrieval.wordpress.com/2017/06/21/improving-essentia-ace-algorithms/); [seffka/ACE2017](https://github.com/seffka/ACE2017)
- Same blog, the failed and the working HMM settings, in the author’s numbers. A stay of 0.75 and a jump mass of 0.25 on every beat, combined with a root-interval matrix from the De Clercq and Temperley rock corpus, dropped accuracy to 48.4%. Flattening that matrix by exponent `k` (`P_ij` pulled toward `1/24` by `(P_ij − 1/24)^k`) and shortening the Hanning window, the best pair he reports is a 1.3 second window and `k = 1.055`, at 80.50%. He writes that the 0.75 model is wrong because “beats have hierarchy and chord changes [go] on strong beat mainly,” and that he did not put that hierarchy in. He also cites Cho and Bello: smoothing the beat-synchronous chromagram can hurt an HMM that already supplies the duration prior. — [Eremenko, 21 June 2017](https://musicinformationretrieval.wordpress.com/2017/06/21/improving-essentia-ace-algorithms/)
- GitHub anecdote, madmom maintainers, issue 403. A user measured chord changes about 0.11 s after the downbeat on US pop, and “the downbeat chord is misclassified around 10% of the time.” Sebastian Böck’s reply: chord recognition runs at 10 fps and downbeat tracking at 100 fps, so one frame is already ~0.1 s; cymbal energy on the downbeat may also delay the chord posterior. Filip Korzeniowski: for MIREX he “align[ed] the chord transitions to the next beat time” and “results improved a tiny bit”; he had not merged that code. Böck’s suggested alternative, not a shipped parameter: weight downbeats so a change is attracted to the downbeat rather than to the nearest beat, and do a coarse downbeat alignment before the beat-level one. — [CPJKU/madmom issue 403](https://github.com/CPJKU/madmom/issues/403)
- GitHub anecdote, madmom discussion 468. A user asked for a sensitivity knob because some files produced too many chord changes and some too few, using `DeepChromaChordRecognitionProcessor`. The reply: both the chroma net and the chord net are learned, and there is no single parameter that targets change rate. No numeric fix is given. — [CPJKU/madmom discussion 468](https://github.com/CPJKU/madmom/discussions/468)
- GitHub anecdote, madmom issue 366, about the bar grid rather than the chord label. For a downbeat track that would not sit still, the maintainer suggested `max_bpm` around 100, or `transition_lambda` of 1000 to prefer constant tempo, or a different network in `DOWNBEATS_BLSTM`. A commenter reported that `observation_lambda=300` held the tempo in their file. Those numbers are one-file experiments. Discussion 503 repeats the same advice and adds clipping the activation to 0..0.5 before the DBN. — [issue 366](https://github.com/CPJKU/madmom/issues/366); [discussion 503](https://github.com/CPJKU/madmom/discussions/503)
- GitHub anecdote, ChordMiniApp discussion 123. The author says chord-to-measure alignment was unstable versus Song Master Pro. Concrete heuristics they were testing: take the most common downbeat spacing as the meter; choose either the Beat Transformer timeline or madmom’s BPM spacing; snap a chord that straddles two beats to the nearest beat (they say this lags, and that Beat Transformer lagged more than madmom); then shift the whole bar’s chords by an integer beat in `[0, meter−1]` to maximise how many changes land on downbeats. They say they do not trust the model’s own downbeats when bar lengths come out as 5 instead of 6. — [ChordMiniApp discussion 123](https://github.com/ptnghia-j/ChordMiniApp/discussions/123)

### Inferences
- “A new chord on every beat” is what `ChordsDetectionBeats` does by construction: one independent argmax per tick. The fixes that moved the 56-song blog score are (1) a 1.3–2.0 s chroma smooth before the per-beat sample and (2) an HMM whose off-diagonal is close to uniform. Snapping a boundary onto the next beat is a one-line post-process and, in the only A/B a madmom author reported, a small gain.
- A practical NumPy/SciPy stack that matches those reports, without a trained chord net: spectral-flux or library beats in, Hanning smooth of chroma (~1.3–2 s in the blog; 2 s is also essentia’s default chord window), one chroma sample per beat, Viterbi with a high stay (Chordino’s 0.99 is per frame, so it must be restated per beat; Mauch’s 0.5/0.9/0.6/0.9 already is per beat). madmom’s chord CNN is the path discussion 468 says you cannot retune with one knob.

### Gaps
- Searches aimed at Music Stack Exchange, Reddit (`r/audioengineering`, `r/WeAreTheMusicMakers`, `r/AudioProgramming`), and Kaggle did not return a thread in which someone posts a self-transition, a window length, or a beat-strength weight as the fix for a per-beat chord flood. The Stack Exchange hits are about human transcription (below), not about an ACE bug. Kaggle threads with parameter dumps were not found. The practitioner record that does name numbers is GitHub issues plus one MIR blog.
- The 80.5% blog figure is 56 Beatles tracks chosen by the author, with a note that the gain over Chordino was about 1 point and “perhaps because of overfitting.” It is not a MIREX number.

## How should a seventh that appears only on beats 3 and 4 be written?

### Takeaway
Write two symbols, `A` then `A7`, when beat 3 is a new harmonic region. Keep a single `A` when the seventh is a melodic tone over an unchanged bass and root. The published models already disagree on purpose: Mauch’s change weights make beat 3 the legal place for that split and treat `7` as its own chord class, while the 2012 MIREX-style score folds dominant sevenths back into major.

### Cited Findings
- Berklee lead-sheet practice: a chord symbol is the current harmonic region and “stays in effect until the chord changes.” It is placed above the staff, centered on the beat where the new region begins. `C7` means dominant seventh; major seventh needs `Maj7` or `Ma7`. This is a notation rule, not an audio result. Applied to a bar whose sonority is A on beats 1–2 and A7 on beats 3–4, the rule puts `A` on beat 1 and `A7` on beat 3. — [Berklee Today, “Why Lead Sheets?”](https://www.berklee.edu/berklee-today/summer-2018/lead-sheet)
- Mauch’s thesis treats the seventh as a different state, not as noise on the triad. In the key model, `Gmaj7` is a tonic chord in G major and `G7` is not, because a tonic must use only key pitch classes and share the key root. The `full` vocabulary gives `7` and `maj7` their own 12 transpositions inside the 121. Because `a_3 = 0.4` and `a_2 = a_4 = 0.1`, a change from `A` to `A7` exactly on beat 3 is the change the prior allows; the same change on beat 2 or 4 is down-weighted like any other flicker. — [Mauch thesis, §§4.2.3 and 4.3.1](https://webspace.eecs.qmul.ac.uk/s.e.dixon/phd/MatthiasMauch-PhD-Thesis.pdf)
- de Haas et al. keep dominant seventh as a separate template in the 48-chord audio dictionary, so the decoder can emit it. For the published score they then collapse it: “chords of the major and dominant class are classified as major.” That collapse is an evaluation map onto 25 classes, not a statement that the chart should show one symbol. Their merge rule would still join beat 3 with beats 1–2 when `A` and `A7` both survive in both candidate lists and the left list is on beat 1; it would keep the split when the intersection loses `A`. — [ISMIR 2012, §§3.2, 3.5, 4](https://ismir2012.ismir.net/event/papers/295_ISMIR_2012.pdf)
- Music Stack Exchange is anecdote from working musicians, and it is about passing tones rather than about this seventh. One answer (Tim): a sonority that lasts a whole bar is more than a passing chord and can take its own label; a one-beat difference is often written, and is also the detail simplified charts drop. Another answer: a rough harmonic rhythm is two chords per bar at the fast end and one chord per two bars at the slow end; prefer the simplest function; follow the bass. Their example collapses `Dm`–`Dsus2` to `Dm` because the root and the bass do not move and `sus2` is a melodic decoration. A third answer: in 4/4 the strong beats are 1 and 3, and a one-chord-per-bar reduction should be chosen from the notes on those beats. — [Passing note vs changed chord](https://music.stackexchange.com/questions/98995/passing-note-vs-changed-chord); [Chords from melody notes](https://music.stackexchange.com/questions/5348/is-it-possible-to-find-the-correct-chords-from-melody-notes-alone)

### Inferences
- The decision is a vocabulary decision, then a beat-strength decision. If `A7` is in the dictionary and the seventh is actually present for half a bar, Mauch’s weights and Berklee’s “new region on this beat” rule agree: print `A` on beat 1 and `A7` on beat 3. Collapsing to `A` matches the MIREX major/minor map and the Stack Exchange advice for same-root decoration, and it throws away a dominant that is about to resolve (the case the grammar papers bother to model).
- Do not collapse because beats 3 and 4 flickered through a seventh for a frame or two. That is the Chordino 0.99 / gamma-duration problem. Collapse when the seventh never wins a beat-length chroma sample on a strong beat, or when the bass stays on A and the product only wants triads.
- A NumPy rule that follows the published weights, once beat-synchronous posteriors `p_t(c)` exist:

```text
a = {1: 0.5, 2: 0.1, 3: 0.4, 4: 0.1}
stay = 1 - a[beat_in_bar]
switch = a[beat_in_bar] / (N_C - 1)
# Viterbi local cost: log p_t(c) + log(stay if c == prev else switch)
```

  For the seventh question, run it with `A` and `A7` as different states. The path prints both symbols only when beat 3’s emission for `A7` pays for `log(0.4/120)` against `log(0.6)` in the 121-chord set, or for `log(0.4/47)` against `log(0.6)` in the 48-chord set. On beats 2 and 4 the stay term is `log(0.9)`, so the same seventh flicker loses.

### Gaps
- No annotation guide in the Isophonics / MIREX material read here states a house rule for “seventh on beats 3 and 4 only.” The Berklee page is pedagogy. The Stack Exchange thread never mentions a half-bar dominant seventh.
- Mauch does not publish a special `A`→`A7` probability. Equation 4.5 uses the same `a` for every pair of distinct labels. A larger prior just for “add a seventh” would be an extension, not a published parameter.
